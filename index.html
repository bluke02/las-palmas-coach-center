Your first 7 moves

1. Get cleared

Complete Las Palmas volunteer registration, screening, and assigned safety training before working with players. Use the buddy system and avoid prohibited one-on-one interactions.

2. Read the current rules

Install the free Little League Rulebook App. Review the rulebook for your exact program and division, plus Las Palmas ground rules.

3. Build your staff

Recruit approved assistants: an on-field helper, scorekeeper or pitch counter, dugout coordinator, and team parent.

4. Know every player

Learn names, experience, medical or accessibility information voluntarily shared with the league, throwing hand, and one personal goal.

5. Publish expectations

Share arrival time, attendance process, equipment list, weather plan, sportsmanship standard, and communication channel.

6. Plan every minute

Use small stations, short explanations, many repetitions, water breaks, and a fun finish. Avoid long lines.

7. Define success

Measure improvement, courage, effort, teamwork, safety, and love of the game, not only wins.

Game-day safety and administration

  • Inspect the field, bases, backstop, bats, helmets, catcher equipment, and first-aid supplies before play.
  • Confirm the lineup, available players, mandatory-play plan, pitch-count or softball innings tracker, and eligible pitchers.
  • Use only league-approved adults in player areas. Keep the dugout organized and players supervised.
  • Stop for unsafe conditions and follow the league's weather, lightning, heat, injury, and emergency procedures.
  • Do not argue judgment calls. Ask rule questions calmly through the manager and model respect.
  • After the game, reconcile pitching records, report injuries or incidents, tidy the area, and send the next-event reminder.

Program and division guide

Important: “Minors A” and “Minors B” are local labels, not separate Little League International divisions. The coaching targets below are a practical Las Palmas draft. The Las Palmas Board must confirm local pitching format, run limits, stealing, bunting, game length, batting order, and any special ground rules before publication.

Rules every coach should track

Mandatory play

Little League states that each rostered player present at the start of a regular-season game must play at least six defensive outs and complete at least one time at bat. Shortened games and certain large-roster Minor/Tee Ball options can affect administration, so verify the current rulebook and local adoption.

Pitching eligibility

Baseball uses age-based pitch limits and rest thresholds. Softball uses innings pitched and rest rules. Assign a dedicated tracker and verify eligibility before every game.

Pitcher and catcher workload

Baseball includes restrictions when a player catches four or more innings and limits returning to catcher after set pitching workloads. Check Regulation VI before assigning both positions on one day.

Local options

Minor League can be coach pitch, machine pitch, or player pitch. Local leagues also choose certain roster, batting, run-limit, and advancement options. Never assume last season’s format.

Equipment

Use the current bat standard and division-appropriate balls. Inspect helmets and catcher gear. Remove any item the umpire determines presents a hazard.

When unsure

Pause, ask the umpire for the applicable rule, and consult the current Rulebook App after the game. This page is a coaching aid, not a substitute for the official rulebook.

A repeatable 75-minute practice

0–8 min
Welcome + movement: names, one goal, dynamic movement, base-running game.
8–18 min
Throwing: partner progression, grip, target, feet, short distances first.
18–48 min
Three stations: hitting, ground balls, throwing/catching. Rotate every 9–10 minutes.
48–63 min
Team skill: cutoffs, force plays, backing up, bunt defense, or first-and-third concepts appropriate to the division.
63–72 min
Game challenge: clean-fielding streak, bucket throws, situational points, or controlled scrimmage.
72–75 min
Close: effort shout-outs, one lesson, equipment count, next-event reminder.

Station rule: aim for one coach or approved adult per station, no more than 3–5 players waiting, and a ball or task in every player’s hands whenever safe.

Drill library

Hit the bucket

Skill: throwing accuracy. Place a bucket or target 20–50 feet away. Players earn team points for accurate throws. Adjust distance, ball type, and target size.

Four corners

Skill: catch, transfer, footwork. Players throw around a square. Add direction changes, ground-ball entry, or a timed clean circuit.

Ground-ball gates

Skill: fielding posture and funnel. Roll balls through cone gates. Player fields, sets feet, and throws to a target.

Tee zones

Skill: bat path and contact. Use a tee and three field zones. Award points for hard contact through the chosen zone, not distance.

Base-running traffic lights

Skill: listening and body control. Green means run, yellow athletic stop, red freeze. Add first-base run-through and rounding progressions.

21 outs

Skill: team defense. The group earns an “out” for a clean field-and-throw sequence and restarts or loses a point after an error. Keep feedback positive.

Fly-ball communication

Skill: priority and voice. Begin with soft toss or tennis balls. Require a loud call and a backup player on every repetition.

Chaos catch

Skill: reaction and tracking. In pairs, coach points left/right or calls a color cone before a safe toss. Scale speed to age and confidence.

Mini game

Skill: decision-making. Play 3v3 or 4v4 in a reduced area with one teaching rule, such as force at first or hitting behind the runner.

Team parent and family support

Use “team parent” or “family coordinator” as an inclusive role title. The family coordinator reduces administrative load but does not replace the manager’s authority, safety duties, or private handling of player concerns.

10-minute parent meeting agenda

  1. Coach philosophy: development, safety, belonging, and fun.
  2. Calendar, arrival time, attendance, pickup, and weather channel.
  3. Player equipment and uniform expectations.
  4. Family roles and volunteer clearance.
  5. Sportsmanship and communication, including a 24-hour cooling-off practice for non-safety concerns.
  6. How concerns, injuries, accessibility needs, and confidential matters should be shared.

First message to families

Welcome to Las Palmas Little League! Our goals are to keep every player safe, teach fundamentals, build confident teammates, and make the season fun. Please reply with your preferred contact information, any attendance conflicts, and any information the league has asked coaches to collect. Our first practice will include a short family meeting and volunteer sign-up.

Practice planning checklist

Goal selected · station leaders assigned · equipment packed · field and weather checked · water breaks scheduled · modifications planned · emergency contacts accessible · fun finish chosen · pickup supervision assigned.

Game lineup checklist

Attendance confirmed · starting positions · batting order · mandatory-play map · eligible pitchers · catcher workload · scorekeeper · pitch/innings tracker · dugout adult · equipment inspection · postgame record reconciliation.

Positive coaching phrases

“Great decision.” “Reset and win the next pitch.” “Show me ready feet.” “What did you see?” “That was brave.” “Help the teammate behind you.” “Errors are information.”
