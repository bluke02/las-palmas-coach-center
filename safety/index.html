Safe, Organized Game Days

Practical, player-first support for volunteer baseball and softball coaches.

Game-day checklist

A safe game day is planned before players arrive, actively supervised while play is underway, and properly closed out afterward. Assign each task to a named adult so nothing depends on memory or assumptions.

Before first pitch

Inspect and prepare

  • Walk the entire playing area: look for holes, standing water, loose bases, exposed sprinkler heads, rocks, broken glass, damaged fencing, and trip hazards.
  • Check that bases, pitching equipment, backstop, fences, and protective screens are secured and placed for the division.
  • Confirm the field is playable after rain, heat, or wind. Pause setup and contact the field coordinator when conditions are uncertain.
  • Inspect helmets, catcher masks, bats, balls, and first-aid supplies. Remove cracked or loose equipment from service.
  • Confirm attendance, emergency contacts, approved helpers, player eligibility, and the lineup or participation plan.
  • Identify the nearest first-aid supplies, phone, restrooms, shade, water, and emergency access point; tell the other adults where they are.

Best practice

Use a repeatable walk-through: dugouts, foul territory, bases, infield, pitcher area, outfield, fences, and spectator boundary. Photograph or report hazards before asking players to enter.

During play

Supervise and communicate

  • Keep players in the dugout or assigned warm-up area, away from live-ball lanes and the on-deck area unless the league permits otherwise.
  • Require helmets for batters and runners whenever required, and keep bats in the designated rack or controlled area.
  • Track pitch counts, catcher innings, mandatory play, substitutions, and injuries as the game changes.
  • Schedule water breaks and watch for heat illness, unusual fatigue, dizziness, confusion, breathing trouble, or a player who stops responding normally.
  • Stop activity for lightning, unsafe field conditions, equipment failure, or an injured player. Keep other players back and let the umpire or league official restart play.
  • Use calm, specific communication: identify the hazard, move players to safety, notify the coach or official, and document what happened.

Best practice

Use a buddy system for younger players: one adult leads instruction or the game while another watches the dugout, bench, and waiting players.

Injury response

Protect the player first

  • Stop play and create space. Do not move a player with a possible head, neck, or serious injury unless there is immediate danger.
  • Notify the manager and parent or guardian according to league procedure. Call emergency services for life-threatening symptoms or when directed by trained personnel.
  • Do not diagnose, give medication, or return a player to play without the required evaluation and approval.
  • Record the time, activity, observed symptoms, adults present, and actions taken using the league's incident process.
  • For suspected concussion, breathing difficulty, severe bleeding, loss of consciousness, or worsening symptoms, keep the player out and seek professional medical care.

Best practice

Use only first-aid steps within your training. A volunteer's role is to protect, notify, and document - not to decide that an injury is minor.

After the game

Close out the field

  • Confirm every player has been released to an authorized adult; never assume a child has a ride.
  • Reconcile the scorebook, pitch counts, catcher innings, substitutions, and any mandatory-play notes while details are fresh.
  • Collect helmets, bats, catcher gear, bases, screens, balls, first-aid supplies, keys, and team documents.
  • Walk the field and dugouts again, remove trash, report damage, and leave gates, storage, and lights in the league-approved condition.
  • Share only necessary follow-up information through approved channels and report incidents or unsafe conditions promptly.

Best practice

Use a two-adult closeout: one adult checks players and records while the other checks equipment, field condition, and facilities.

Stop and ask for help: When a rule, injury, weather condition, equipment issue, or field hazard is beyond your training or authority, pause play and contact the umpire, manager, league official, field coordinator, or emergency services as appropriate.
