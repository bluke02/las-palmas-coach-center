Families Make the Team Work

Practical, player-first support for volunteer baseball and softball coaches.

Helpful roles

Strong teams share the work. Choose a role that fits your time and follow the manager's plan, league volunteer requirements, and safety policies. Coaches remain responsible for player instruction and game decisions; approved helpers support the team without replacing the coach or umpire.

Family coordinator

Keep families informed and make volunteer coverage predictable.

Example duties

  • Send practice and game reminders with arrival times, locations, and what players should bring.
  • Maintain the contact list and note availability, access needs, and schedule conflicts.
  • Build a rotating snack, carpool, or volunteer schedule.
  • Share approved league updates without changing coach or league instructions.

Dugout helper

Support the manager with organization, hydration, and a calm dugout after approval.

Example duties

  • Check that players have water, gloves, helmets, and required equipment.
  • Help players stay seated, attentive, and ready for their next turn.
  • Track who is batting or entering the field when the coach asks.
  • Alert the coach to an injury, missing equipment, or player concern; do not provide treatment beyond your training.

Only approved and properly screened volunteers should work inside the dugout.

Scorekeeper

Maintain the game's official record and give the coaching staff reliable information.

Example duties

  • Record the batting order, outs, runs, hits, substitutions, and defensive changes.
  • Track pitcher pitches and catcher innings when assigned by the league.
  • Confirm unclear substitutions or scoring plays with the umpire or manager.
  • Keep the book legible and reconcile the score after each half-inning.

Game field setup lead

Prepare a safe, playable field before the first pitch and restore it after the game.

Example duties

  • Arrive early with the field key, approved equipment, and the league's field checklist.
  • Inspect the infield, warning track, fences, bases, pitching area, backstop, and dugouts for hazards.
  • Set bases and pitching equipment at the division's approved distances; confirm Minors B's 42-foot pitching distance when applicable.
  • Drag or rake only as permitted, chalk or line the field, place protective screens, and secure loose equipment.
  • Remove trash, return gear, close gates, and report damage or unsafe conditions to the league.

Do not alter a field or begin play when conditions are unsafe. Ask the umpire or league field coordinator for a ruling.

Equipment manager

Keep shared equipment organized, safe, and ready for practice or games.

Example duties

  • Inventory balls, tees, nets, bases, helmets, catcher gear, and first-aid supplies.
  • Check for cracked helmets, damaged bats, loose masks, or missing fasteners before use.
  • Label team equipment and collect it after each practice or game.
  • Report replacement needs instead of allowing unsafe equipment onto the field.

Team photographer or communicator

Celebrate the team while respecting privacy, league policy, and family preferences.

Example duties

  • Collect photo permissions before sharing player images.
  • Share positive game updates, schedules, and team milestones through the approved channel.
  • Never publish medical information, private contact details, or sensitive player situations.
  • Direct rule questions, complaints, and playing-time concerns to the manager or league officials.
Good volunteer habits: Be on time, follow the coach's plan, keep player information private, model sportsmanship, and speak up immediately about safety concerns.
