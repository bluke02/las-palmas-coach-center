Pitcher and catcher position changes

These Little League International baseball rules determine whether a player may move between pitcher and catcher during the same day. Track innings caught and pitches delivered independently.

SituationWhat the rule requires
Catcher to pitcherA player who catches four or more innings in a game is not eligible to pitch later that calendar day.
Catcher (age 14 and under) to pitcher, 3 innings or fewer caughtThe player may pitch, but may not return to catcher after delivering 21 or more pitches. If the player reaches 20 pitches while facing a batter, they may finish that batter and retain catcher eligibility until the at-bat or half-inning ends, or they are removed.

Pitcher to catcherIf a player delivers 41 or more pitches, they may not play catcher for the remainder of that day unless covered by the live-batter threshold exception in the official rule. Confirm the applicable age and division rule before moving the player.
Pitcher removed from moundOnce removed from the mound, a pitcher may not return as pitcher. In Intermediate (50/70), Junior, and Senior divisions only, a pitcher who remains in the game may move to another defensive position and return to pitch once later in the game.

Las Palmas Minors B local rules

These rules are specific to the Las Palmas Minors B format shown in the league guide. They supplement, and do not replace, the current Little League rulebook, league policies, or umpire direction.

At-bat and scoring

  • Standard foul-ball and foul-tip rules apply.
  • Standard strike-zone rules apply.
  • Each batter receives five pitches: the first three from the player pitcher and the final two from the coach. If the player pitcher is throwing strikes, allow the player pitcher to try to strike the batter out.
  • There is a five-run maximum per inning.
  • There is no bunting.

Pitching and field setup

  • Player pitchers are limited to 50 pitches per day.
  • Pitching distance is 42 feet from home plate.
  • Coaches must pitch from the mound.
  • After three unintentional hit batters by a player pitcher, remove the player pitcher for safety.
  • Use a maximum of four defensive players in the outfield.

Base running

  • No stealing.
  • Once the ball is returned to the infield and controlled by a defensive player, the runner stops advancing.
  • On an overthrow, the runner may advance one additional base only, unless the ball is actively played.
  • If a batter is hit by a coach-pitched ball, the batter does not take first base and continues the at-bat.

Participation

  • If a team does not have enough players, teams should share or borrow players rather than forfeit whenever possible so the kids can play.

Example lineup card

A lineup card is the coach's game-day control sheet. It records the batting order, defensive positions, starters and substitutes, defensive innings, and notes that affect player eligibility. Prepare it before first pitch, then update it as players enter the game.

Why use one?

  • Plan mandatory play before the final inning.
  • Track who has played defensively and who still needs time.
  • Coordinate pitching, catching, and substitution restrictions.
  • Give coaches and the scorekeeper a shared reference.
  • Reduce confusion when the game changes quickly.

Pregame coach checklist

Players & lineup

  • Confirm attendance.
  • Complete the batting order.
  • Assign defensive positions.
  • Identify emergency substitutes.

Pitchers & catchers

  • Confirm pitcher eligibility and rest.
  • Review pitch-count limits.
  • Review catcher-to-pitcher restrictions.
  • Inspect catcher equipment.

Rules & safety

  • Know time, run-limit, mercy-rule, and curfew procedures.
  • Confirm stealing and local modifications.
  • Have first-aid supplies available.
  • Inspect the playing surface and supervise the dugout.

The Las Palmas standard

Every coach should leave the field knowing every player was respected, developed, safe, and given an opportunity to compete. Winning is a goal; developing players is the mission.
